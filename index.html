SPHEREx Blink

Flip through infrared images of the same patch of sky, taken months apart. Anything that moves jumps between frames.

How to use this

Press Play and watch one spot in the sky. Stars that stay put stay put. A nearby star, brown dwarf, comet or asteroid shifts a little from frame to frame.

Use Back and Next, or the left and right arrow keys, to step through one frame at a time. The space bar pauses and plays. Raise the brightness or contrast to see faint things, or invert the picture.

Each frame is a small cutout from one SPHEREx exposure. The date above the picture is when that exposure was taken. The cutouts are centered on where the target is expected to be on the observation dates.

This publication makes use of data products from the Spectro-Photometer for the History of the Universe, Epoch of Reionization and Ices Explorer (SPHEREx), which is a joint project of the Jet Propulsion Laboratory and the California Institute of Technology, and is funded by the National Aeronautics and Space Administration.

Images come from the NASA/IPAC Infrared Science Archive (IRSA). Built for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026.
